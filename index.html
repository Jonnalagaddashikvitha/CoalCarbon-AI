Project summary

CoalCarbon AI is an ML-powered decision-support system for coal mine decarbonisation. It uses historical coal production data to forecast future production using machine-learning regression models, compares model performance using rolling-origin validation, estimates carbon emissions using standard emission factors, and allows users to simulate decarbonisation strategies such as renewable energy, diesel reduction and equipment electrification. Supporting ML modules perform emission-intensity prediction, high-emitter classification and mine clustering. Since actual mine-level operational data is not publicly available, synthetic data is used only for demonstrating mine-level ML workflows.

🧬Machine Learning Pipeline

From real data to a validated forecast — every stage is implemented in coalcarbon_ml.py; this page only displays its output (ml_results.json).

1 · Data CollectionHistorical state-wise coal production (Ministry of Coal / PIB) loaded from a CSV.
2 · Data CleaningWide table → one row per state-year; units (Mt) and years checked.
3 · Feature EngineeringPrevious-year production, year index and state become ML inputs; target is growth vs last year.
4 · Train/Test SplitTime-ordered: train only on earlier years, test on the next year. No shuffling.
5 · Model TrainingRandom Forest, Gradient Boosting and Ridge are fitted on the training window.
6 · Model EvaluationPredictions compared with actuals using MAE, RMSE, MAPE and R².
7 · Best Model SelectionThe model with the lowest MAPE is chosen and compared with a naive baseline.
8 · PredictionThe chosen model is refitted on all data and forecasts future years with ~80% bands.

Why Random Forest?

Random Forest combines many decision trees and can capture nonlinear relationships between input features and coal production. It is also less sensitive to individual noisy observations than a single decision tree.

⚠️Limitations

Stated openly so the results are not over-claimed.

  1. Only five historical years (FY2019-20 to FY2023-24) are available for state-level forecasting; validation uses just 14 predictions (2 test years × 7 states).
  2. Mine-level operational data such as actual diesel consumption and equipment activity is not publicly available.
  3. Therefore the mine-level ML experiments (emission intensity, high-emitter classification, clustering) use synthetic data.
  4. Synthetic data validates the ML pipeline but cannot be treated as real-world mine findings.
  5. Forecast results — and their uncertainty bands — are indicative because of the limited historical sample. The model has not been refreshed with FY2024-25 actuals.
  6. Actual mine deployment requires real mine operational data.
  7. The carbon calculator and scenario simulator are emission-factor and rule-based arithmetic, not machine learning.

Why this matters for India

Coal still powers most of India's electricity, and mines are under growing pressure to report and cut emissions. A self-serve tool lets a mine move from "we don't know our number" to "here is our plan" in minutes, not months.

❓Frequently Asked

Where do the emission factors come from?

Diesel and fugitive methane follow IPCC 2006 Tier 1 defaults; grid electricity follows CEA's CO₂ Baseline Database (v22.0); transport follows a DEFRA/GLEC-style road-freight average. Full citations are in Data & Sources below. Every factor is still an editable input so a mine can swap in its own certified figures.

Is the coal-production data real?

Yes — the state-wise production figures (FY2019-20 to FY2023-24) in the AI Forecast and State-wise Comparison are Ministry of Coal / PIB figures, cited below. The mine-specific activity inputs in the Calculator (diesel litres, electricity kWh, etc.) are still placeholders for you to replace with your own mine's data.

What does the AI forecast actually predict?

It predicts next years' coal production from the real historical trend for the selected state. A Random Forest trained in Python on the real Ministry of Coal series, chosen over baselines by rolling-origin validation (see ML Lab). With only five years per state, treat long-horizon forecasts and their bands as indicative.

What is "tCO₂e"?

Tonnes of carbon-dioxide equivalent — a common unit that converts every greenhouse gas (CO₂, methane, etc.) into the amount of CO₂ that would cause the same warming, so different sources can be added together.

Can this replace an official carbon audit?

No — it is a decision-support and planning tool. Official reporting still needs certified factors, verified activity data and, usually, third-party audit.
